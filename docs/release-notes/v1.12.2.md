# Kumo 1.12.2

## What's new

- **Crash reports.** If something breaks, Kumo can send a report with the same switch as the anonymous usage count (*Settings > App settings*; off means nothing is queued or sent). It holds the error and where it happened in the code, the Kumo and system version, the device maker and model, the page you were on, whether the VPN was connected and the last lines of Kumo's own log. Links, IP addresses, e-mail addresses, ids, tokens, passwords, the names of your servers and subscriptions, and your user name in folder paths are removed before it is saved or sent. At most 10 a day. The details are in the [privacy policy](https://github.com/AbolDev/kumo-client/blob/main/PRIVACY.md).
- **Open links and files with Kumo.** `vless://`, `vmess://`, `trojan://`, `ss://`, `hysteria2://` and the other server links, subscription links (`clash://`, `sing-box://`, `hiddify://`), shared text and `.json`, `.yaml` and `.ovpn` files can be opened with Kumo from a browser or a chat (on Android also from the *Share* menu). Kumo always asks before it adds anything. On Windows the installer has a task for it (on by default), on Linux the packages list the links, and a copy of Kumo that is already running takes the link. On macOS the system hands them over through the app itself (links and .ovpn, .json, .yaml files).
- **User-Agent and headers for a subscription.** *Edit subscription > Advanced*: some providers answer only one kind of client or want a key in a header. Headers go only to the subscription's own address and are also used by the background update on Android.
- **Find the best settings for my network** (*Home menu > Troubleshoot*). Kumo tries a few options (splitting the TLS hello, a browser fingerprint, multiplexing) on up to three of your VLESS, VMess or Trojan servers and shows which work best on your network. It applies a better one only when you tap *Use it*, and you can undo it. It runs on your device only and sends nothing. Name lookups (DNS) are not part of it.
- **WARP: find the best way to connect, and Warp-in-Warp.** On the WARP page, *Find the best way* tries every method, address and port of the profile and switches to the fastest one that answers. *Warp-in-Warp* (experimental) carries one WARP tunnel inside a second WARP account's tunnel; it has not been proven on every network. *Scan more addresses* also tries addresses from Cloudflare's WARP ranges that are not in the profile (the profile can name its own ranges and ports).
- **Server names are looked up over encrypted DNS first.** Before it connects, Kumo looks up the name of the server over encrypted DNS (Cloudflare and Google at once; while Kumo is open and not connected this is done ahead, so a connection finds the address ready). Your network's resolver then cannot see which server you look for, or hand out a wrong address (a common way of blocking a server). If those two are blocked, it asks the system's own resolver as before, so the connection still works. It is on by default and can be turned off in *Settings > Network*; *Strict DNS* is unchanged. Cloudflare and Google see the server's name.
- **Android kill switch.** With the kill switch on, the internet now stays blocked when the sing-box core stops by itself, or when a reconnect of a connection that was up fails (before, only when Xray stopped).
- **Android Always-on VPN.** When the system starts Kumo itself (*Always-on VPN* in Android's VPN settings, with or without *Block connections without VPN*), Kumo now connects again with the last connection instead of ignoring the request.
- **Find a clean IP** (*Home menu > Find a clean IP*, and *Edit config > Find a clean IP* for VLESS, VMess and Trojan servers behind a CDN). Kumo tries up to 10,000 addresses of Cloudflare (its current ranges), Fastly or your own list (addresses, ranges, CIDR, domain names, or a file) and shows the fastest ones, either with a server's own settings or by themselves on the ports you choose. The page has a setup, a scan and a results view: you pick how many addresses (200 to 10,000) and the timeout with buttons, sort the results by speed, loss or download speed, see good addresses while the scan runs, stop after enough of them, measure loss and jitter, try the neighbors of the best, split the TLS hello, use *Gentle mode* for networks that cut the connection when a scan looks like a flood, skip the first stage, see Cloudflare's data center and test the download speed, resume a stopped scan, and copy the results as `IP:port`, links or a subscription, or tick the ones you want and add them to one or more of your configs (as copies in a group of their own, *Clean IPs*, which the next scan replaces) or put one in place of a config's address. It runs on your device only.
- **A stronger ad blocker** (*Settings > Network > Block ads and trackers*). A third level, *Maximum*, merges the biggest public lists (HaGeZi Ultimate, OISD Big, the AdGuard DNS filter, PersianBlocker, and HaGeZi's lists of pop-up ads and of the trackers of phone and TV makers) into one; it uses more memory, and now and then a site needs an exception. The lists are read in full, so a list that names a whole domain now blocks everything under it. A list of about 100,000 names is built into the app, so ads are blocked from the first connection even when the lists cannot be downloaded. The lists are refreshed every day from more mirrors, and Settings shows how many lists and names are in use and when they were updated, with *Update now*. *Never block* takes the sites and apps that ad blocking should leave alone. At the strong and maximum levels, apps can no longer get around the blocker by calling a public encrypted DNS server by its address. Two optional switches hide cookie banners and block affiliate links. Names that apps need are never blocked, even when a list has them: the Facebook and Instagram API, YouTube, the players of video sites, app links, newsletter links, support chat.
- **Faster to connect and to disconnect.** A connection no longer waits for any list to download: the lists there are used (the lists of *Direct countries* and an ad list now come with the app), and they are refreshed a few seconds after the connection is up, through it. Lists are read and merged in the background, so the screen does not stop, and the big ones are kept in sing-box's binary format, which the core reads several times faster. Disconnecting takes at most a moment: it no longer waits for open connections to close or for tests in progress, and on Windows the system proxy is set through the registry directly instead of a program for each value.
- **A new look for the home page, settings and the logs.** Tools in a page of their own; the progress of a test under each group; swipe a server to delete (with *Undo*) or share, press and hold to pick several; three big buttons when there is no server; Network settings in five sections, each with *Reset this section*; system colors or Kumo's teal, pure black in dark mode, vibration feedback (phone); Vazirmatn for Persian and Arabic; search, *Only problems* and *Copy for a bug report* in the logs. The notes of an update are shown with their formatting.
- **Find a clean IP can measure speed.** With a server, the best addresses are measured for download and/or upload speed through it (at most the data you pick for each; a slow upload shows the speed it reached), shown as they come, and can be sorted by it. The scan shows its steps, about how long is left and what it is doing.
- **A speed test** (*Tools > Speed test*): download, upload and delay, through the VPN or without it, against Cloudflare, LibreSpeed or OVH, with the speed shown live on a ring, a limit of data (50 MB by default), *Share* and the last 30 tests kept on your device.
- **New Android widgets:** a power button, a quick bar and a status card, small by default and resizable, in the phone's light and dark and the wallpaper's colours; the quick settings tile shows the server.
- **Why a connection failed** is said in a few words under the status.
- **Anti-filter settings for a server** (*Edit config*): fingerprint (with `unsafe`), ALPN, final mask and cipher suites; such a server runs on Xray.
- **The ad lists come through the connection** (a computer in the proxy mode, a phone with Xray), with a setting for it, and a switch for *Block public encrypted DNS*.
- **Slow networks and reconnecting.** On a slow network (H+, a weak signal) the time limits of the tests and of the check of a running connection grow, so working servers are not called dead. A connection no longer waits 2.5 seconds for encrypted DNS that is blocked, and on Android a new connection no longer fails with "address already in use" while the last one is still closing.
- **Share Kumo** (*Settings > About*): a message ready to send in the language you pick, and a QR code of Kumo's site. The notes of an update can be read in another language, and the question before a real delay test is a sheet at the bottom that can remember your answer.
- **Fixes from the crash reports:** a server with a pinned certificate on Xray, a server with a wrong Reality key in *the fastest servers*, the background update of a huge subscription on Android, and a hint in the server list on Windows. Errors of the network itself are no longer sent as crash reports.
- **Android TV.** Two panes, a clear focus ring, the remote starts on the power button, and *Add from your phone* in place of the camera and the file picker.
- **Sync devices** (*Settings > Backup*). Servers, clean IPs and subscriptions (and, if you want, appearance and behaviour settings) move to another device of yours over the local network, sealed with a key that only a one-time code holds. Nothing is applied without a question, and by default it is only added.
- **Disguise and erase** (Android). Kumo can show as an everyday app (or with your own name and icon, or with no icon), and *Erase all data* deletes everything it keeps, after a question and a three-second hold.
- **Safety check of a server.** Well-known sites are fetched through the server and compared with what your network gets, to find a server that changes what passes through it. A server found changing traffic is left out of the automatic mode.
- **A faster first connection in the automatic mode** (the servers are tried with a TCP connect first and the ones that answer go first), **a better routing rules page** (rules chosen from lists of apps, services and sites, with a test, undo, moving and import/export), **redone tools** (service test with the VPN off, live DNS leak test, a clearer *Why can't I connect*, a QR code with Kumo's logo), **no JSON to write for the extra options**, and **a fix for WireGuard servers with no IPv6 address**.
- **Usage count.** The maker and model of the device are now sent from every system (before, only Android), and on Linux the desktop. No serial number or computer name is read.
- **A request for a star.** After a week of use Kumo asks once, and at most a second time a month later, whether you would star the project on GitHub. *Don't ask again* ends it.
